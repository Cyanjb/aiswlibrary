Camera Movements

44 keywords in this category, each one tested and explained in plain language.

Pick keywords as you browse, then press Copy for AI and paste into ChatGPT, Claude or any AI. Copy keywords gives a plain list for Midjourney or Seedance.

Aerial Shot

High drone or helicopter perspective, moving above the world.

Arc Into Crash Zoom

The camera curves slowly around the subject, then zooms in hard and fast.

Arc Shot

The camera curves part of the way around the subject.

Camera Roll

The camera rotates around its own lens, tipping the whole picture over.

Car Reveal One-Take

One unbroken shot that starts from above, drops to the wheel, glides to the lamp, circles to the back and rises to a big wide view.

Chase One-Take

A runner chased down a wet street at night, the camera running ahead, then swinging round to follow.

Crane Shot

The camera sweeps vertically or in an arc from a raised arm, moving between ground level and overhead.

Dolly In

The camera physically glides toward the subject on a track.

Dolly Out

The camera glides away from the subject, revealing context and often leaving them small in their world.

Dolly Zoom

Dolly and zoom in opposite directions: the subject stays put while the background stretches.

FPV Drone

Fast first-person drone flight: the camera is the drone, diving and banking through space.

Fast Orbit Into Crash Zoom

A tight, quick circle around the subject that makes it seem to spin, ending in a hard zoom.

Follow Shot

The camera follows behind someone as they move.

Glide

A slow, weightless drift past the subject, as if carried on a breeze.

Ground Slash

The camera skims just above the ground beside the subject and snaps its aim hard, up and back down.

Handheld

Unstabilized camera with natural shake and drift; human, urgent, imperfect.

Jib Reveal

The camera swoops down from above in one arc, glides along the subject and lands on a detail.

Long Take

One unbroken shot that runs far longer than expected; no cuts, no escape.

Low Dolly-In

The camera moves slowly toward the subject from low down, about table height.

Low Track Into Dutch Angle

A low sideways move along the ground that slowly tips the horizon into a tilt.

Orbit

The camera circles the subject, holding them centered while the world wheels behind.

Pan

The camera stays in one spot and turns left or right, like turning your head.

Pan Onto Subject

The camera turns from somewhere else and comes to rest on your subject.

Pedestal

The whole camera rises or lowers straight up and down, without tilting.

Pedestal Down

The whole camera lowers straight down without tilting.

Push In

A gentle forward drift toward the subject, subtler than a full dolly move.

Rooftop Night Two-Shot

Two people on a rooftop at night, the camera drifting between each face and a shot of both together.

Rush-By Shot

A low camera with a wide lens races right past the subject.

Skim and Barrel Roll

The camera skims just over the subject while rolling a full circle.

Slam Down and Whip

The camera drops fast from high up to the ground, holds for a beat, then whips sideways.

Slider Shot

A short, smooth slide sideways, like the camera is sitting on a little rail.

Snap Zoom

A split-second zoom from wide to tight.

Spiral Shot

The camera circles the subject while rising, like a corkscrew.

Static Hold

The camera does not move at all; the frame is a stage and life happens inside it.

Steadicam

Stabilized floating movement that follows subjects smoothly through space, no track required.

Tabletop Product Shot

A slow push and drift across a styled table that settles on the product.

Three-Person Dialogue Coverage

One take that covers three people talking in a room, moving between each face, over-the-shoulder views and a wide.

Tilt Up

The camera pivots vertically upward, revealing height or hope.

Tracking Shot

The camera travels alongside a moving subject, keeping pace like a companion.

Truck Shot

The whole camera slides sideways past the subject, so near and far things move at different speeds.

Under-Pass Shot

The camera flies low, right under or between the subject, and out the other side.

Undercut Snap

Starts at ankle height, snaps up to the face, glides around, then snaps back down.

Whip Pan

A pan so fast the frame smears; snaps attention from one thing to the next.

Zoom

The camera stays still and only the lens zooms, so the background flattens as it closes in.
