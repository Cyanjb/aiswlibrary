Alexander McQueen

British designer Alexander McQueen (1969-2010) mixed razor-sharp tailoring with dark, romantic drama: tartan, feathers, lace, skulls and runway shows that felt like theater.

Christian Dior

French designer Christian Dior (1905-1957) launched the 1947 New Look: soft shoulders, tiny cinched waists and huge full skirts that brought romance back after the war.

Coco Chanel

French designer Coco Chanel (1883-1971) freed women from corsets with comfortable jersey, collarless tweed suits trimmed in braid, the little black dress and long ropes of pearls.

Cristobal Balenciaga

Spanish designer Cristóbal Balenciaga (1895-1972) shaped fabric like sculpture: balloon skirts, cocoon coats, loose sack dresses and stiff silks that stand away from the body.

Elsa Schiaparelli

Italian-born designer Elsa Schiaparelli (1890-1973) made fashion playful and surreal: shocking pink, trick-of-the-eye sweaters, lobster prints, hats shaped like shoes and wild jeweled buttons.

Halston

American designer Halston (1932-1990) defined 1970s glamour with simple, fluid pieces: jersey halter gowns, caftans, wrap shapes and clean minimal lines that moved on the dance floor.

Madeleine Vionnet

French designer Madeleine Vionnet (1876-1975) mastered the bias cut, cutting fabric on the diagonal so silk gowns drape, cling and flow like water, often with a Greek statue feel.

Yves Saint Laurent

French designer Yves Saint Laurent (1936-2008) put women in tuxedos with his famous Le Smoking suit, and loved safari jackets, sheer blouses and bold blocks of color inspired by art.
