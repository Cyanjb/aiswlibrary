1920s Flapper Dress

Loose, straight dresses that drop to the hips and stop near the knee, covered in beads, sequins or swinging fringe.

1940s Wartime Fashion

Practical style shaped by fabric rationing: padded shoulders, knee-length skirts, neat tailored jackets, headscarves and pinned-up victory roll hairstyles.

1950s New Look

The postwar silhouette made famous by Christian Dior: soft rounded shoulders, a tiny cinched waist and a full skirt that swings below the knee.

1960s Mod Fashion

The bold London look of the mid 1960s: short shift dresses, mini skirts, bright color blocks, black and white graphics, go-go boots and sharp geometric haircuts.

1970s Disco Fashion

Shiny, flowing clothes made for the dance floor: halter jumpsuits, wrap dresses, wide bell bottoms, platform shoes and gold or silver metallic fabric that catches every light.

Edwardian Fashion

Dress from the early 1900s: high lace collars, puffy blouses, long sweeping skirts and enormous hats piled with flowers and feathers.

Elizabethan Fashion

English court dress from the late 1500s: huge starched lace ruffs around the neck, stiff jeweled bodices, wide skirts held out by hoops, and rich brocade sewn with pearls.

Regency Era Fashion

Early 1800s dress with a waistline just under the bust, light white muslin falling straight to the floor, short puffed sleeves, bonnets and little cropped jackets.
