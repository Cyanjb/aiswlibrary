Billy Baldwin

American decorator (1903-1983) known for glossy chocolate brown walls, tall brass bookshelves, simple cotton slipcovers and comfy slipper chairs.

David Hicks

English designer (1929-1998) known for bold geometric carpets, hexagon patterns and daring color clashes like hot pink with orange.

Dorothy Draper

American decorator (1889-1969) known for big, happy glamour: black and white checkerboard floors, huge cabbage rose prints, bright pink and green, and giant white plaster scrolls.

Elsie de Wolfe

American decorator (1859-1950) often called the first professional interior designer.

Florence Knoll

American architect and designer (1917-2019) who shaped the tidy mid-century office: open plans, low sleek sofas, slim steel legs, neat grids and calm blocks of color.

Jean-Michel Frank

French designer (1895-1941) who made quiet luxury: plain rooms in cream and beige, walls of parchment or straw, and chunky simple chairs and tables.

Sister Parish

American decorator (1910-1994) famous for the cozy American country look: patchwork quilts, painted floors, knitted throws, chintz and baskets.

Syrie Maugham

English decorator (1879-1955) famous for her all-white room: white walls, white satin, white flowers and mirrored screens, all glowing together.
