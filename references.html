Film Reference Finder

Free places to find real film frames, and how to turn what you see into keywords. Great directors learned by copying shots they loved. You can too.

How to use a film frame

  1. Find it. Open a camera, lens or lighting keyword and press its StillsLab link, or search one of the sites below.
  2. Name it. Look at three frames and describe them in library words: shot size, angle, lens, light, color. Press + Pick on each keyword.
  3. Prompt it. Press Copy for AI or Copy keywords and write your own scene. Your subject, their technique.

Five-minute drill: open any site below, find a frame you love, and write three keywords for it before you look anything up. Then search this library and check how close you got.

Where to look

Film frames you can search by idea

StillsLab

Frames from films, series, music videos and commercials. Type a plain idea like golden hour or Dutch angle and it filters by shot, light, color and composition. Many frames list the lens and camera too.

Best for

Matching a keyword to real film examples. Every camera, lens and lighting keyword page in this library links here.

Cost

Free to browse and search. Pro is about $7 a month.

Hand-picked stills, one film at a time

FilmGrab

Curated galleries of stills from thousands of films, browsable by director, cinematographer and genre.

Best for

Studying how one film keeps the same look from shot to shot.

Cost

Free. No account needed.

Try this

Open one film and look at 10 frames in a row. Write down what never changes. That list is the film's style formula.

Searchable stills plus short GIF clips, with lots of commercials and music videos alongside films.

Cost

Free trial, then from about $7 a month. Their plans cover non-commercial, reference and educational use only.

Study freely, create your own

Film frames belong to the studios that made them. Use them to learn and for private mood boards. Don't upload them as image references for work you publish or sell, and don't recreate real actors' faces. Describe the look with keywords instead. Each site has its own terms too.
